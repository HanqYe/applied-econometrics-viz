Panel data: each unit belongs to a group Wᵢ — here, its state. That group identity is the fixed effect.
Ignore the groups and fit one line through all points. The pooled slope looks steep.
But the groups sit at different levels. The pooled slope mixes within-group and between-group variation.
Fixed effects split the data by group. Look at each state on its own.
The within transform: subtract each group's mean, Vᵢ = Xᵢ − E[Xᵢ|Wᵢ]. The level differences vanish.
Now regress y on V within each group. A narrow X-spread lets the fit wobble; a wide one holds it steady — each yields a within slope β̂(Wᵢ).
Weight each state by its X-variation — more spread, more weight. Intuition a wide spread makes the fit more stable, so it counts more; State 1 barely at all.
Merge into one cloud. The pooled line (ignoring groups) is steep — the fixed-effects β̂ swings down to the within slope, ruled by the wide-spread states.
